# Question bank content review

Open items found while adding official references to every question (November 2026). Each item names the question, quotes what the official source says, and suggests a fix. **No answer, option or legal statement was changed for any item on this list.** Changing one needs the owner's approval (see AGENTS.md).

Some items are notes rather than problems: they record that only a citation was corrected, or that the official sources were silent and the answer is sound practice. Those are marked "Low priority".

Already fixed with the owner's approval (not listed below): TX fee-06, fee-07, fee-14, rec-04, onl-15, id-04; NY rec-01, id-13, com-10, com-06; CA rec-17, seal-13, spc-03, con-11, com-05; FL fee-09, con-05, con-17, jur-05, onl-02.

## Texas (28 open)

- **tx-com-02** (Commission & Qualifications): How long is a Texas notary public commission?
  Statute: term expires four years after the date the notary QUALIFIES (§406.002; commission effective on qualification, §406.008(a)). The why says "date the commission was issued" and the explanation says "date of appointment". Suggest "four years after the date the notary qualifies".
- **tx-com-04** (Commission & Qualifications): Which of these is an eligibility requirement for an ordinary Texas notary commission?
  The why mentions "escrow officers are a narrow exception". Current §406.004 has no escrow-officer exception. Suggest dropping the parenthetical.
- **tx-com-08** (Commission & Qualifications): Texas requires notary applicants to pass a state-administered written exam before being commissioned.
  Since S.B. 693 (eff. Sept 1, 2025) every applicant and renewing notary must complete the SOS education course AND pass a timed, scored "Notary Education Assessment" before appointment (§406.006(6), §406.023(d); SOS application guide rev. 12/16/2025, p. 3). "Texas has no state notary exam" is now arguably misleading, and the explanation omits the education requirement. Same claim is in tx/state.json notes, seo.js Texas FAQ copy and the seo test "Texas copy never claims a state exam exists". Owner decision needed.
- **tx-com-09** (Commission & Qualifications): Before performing any notarial act, a newly appointed Texas notary must:
  Since Sept 1, 2025 qualification also requires completing the SOS education course (§406.006(6)). Answer still correct; explanation could mention it.
- **tx-com-12** (Commission & Qualifications): What filing fee does the Secretary of State charge with a Texas notary application?
  $21 = $10 commission + $10 bond filing + $1 (§406.007), still correct. Since Sept 2025 the SOS education course carries its own separate, non-refundable fee (§406.023(d)(2)); consider mentioning it.
- **tx-com-15** (Commission & Qualifications): What should a Texas notary do when they move to a new address?
  Statute sets a deadline: notify the SOS within 10 days of the change (§406.019). Explanation could state it.
- **tx-id-01** (Identification): Which list shows the three ways a Texas notary may identify a signer for an acknowledgment?
  Since 2011 a credible witness may be EITHER personally known to the notary OR show the notary their own current government photo ID with signature (§121.005(a)(1)(B)); since June 2025 a current foreign passport is also accepted for residential real estate instruments (§121.005(a)(3)). Correct option and explanation say the witness must be "personally known". Still the best option, but incomplete.
- **tx-id-11** (Identification): The signer's employer phones the notary and vouches for the signer's identity. Does this satisfy Texas identification requirements?
  Why/explanation say a credible witness must be "personally known to the notary"; §121.005(a)(1)(B) also allows a witness who shows their own government photo ID. Answer (No) is still correct.
- **tx-id-13** (Identification): If a credible witness introduces the signer, what must the notary record about the witness?
  §406.014(a)(5) now requires the introducer's "name and mailing address" (wording changed from "residence or alleged residence"). Correct option, why and explanation use the old wording.
- **tx-ack-05** (Acknowledgments): Which short-form certificate fits a corporate officer signing for a company?
  For a corporate officer, §121.008(b)(4) gives a specific corporation form: "...by (name of officer), (title of officer) of (name of corporation) a (state) corporation, on behalf of said corporation." The answer's "representative-capacity" wording "(name) as (title) of (entity)" is the (b)(5) form for public officers, trustees, executors, guardians. Suggest quoting the (b)(4) corporate form.
- **tx-jur-04** (Jurats, Oaths & Affirmations): What is the difference between an oath and an affirmation?
  Cited ch. 602 lists who may give oaths; the oath/affirmation equivalence is §312.011(8) ("Oath" includes affirmation) and the SOS definitions ("Each has the same legal import"). Citation corrected only.
- **tx-jur-05** (Jurats, Oaths & Affirmations): Who decides whether a signer takes an oath or an affirmation?
  No official Texas source says in words that the signer chooses between oath and affirmation; the SOS oath script is "do you swear or affirm". Sound practice, low priority.
- **tx-jur-06** (Jurats, Oaths & Affirmations): What is an affidavit?
  §312.011(1) defines an affidavit as a written statement "signed by the party making it, sworn to before an officer authorized to administer oaths, and officially certified to by the officer under his seal of office". The answer omits "signed" and "certified under seal"; still the best option.
- **tx-jur-10** (Jurats, Oaths & Affirmations): How should a notary properly administer an oath for a jurat?
  SOS training says "While requiring the signer to raise their right hand, you must administer an oath..." and "A gesture such as a nod is not acceptable". The answer and why say raising the hand is optional ("if willing", "customary but optional"). Conflicts with the SOS wording.
- **tx-fee-09** (Fees): Are the amounts in the Texas fee schedule fixed fees that must be charged?
  Also worth noting: §406.024(c) requires the SOS to adjust the fees for inflation every five years, so amounts may change.
- **tx-rec-05** (Recordkeeping): What must the record state about identification?
  Explanation says the introducer's "name and residence"; §406.014(a)(5) now says "name and mailing address".
- **tx-rec-07** (Recordkeeping): What must be recorded about the grantee of an instrument?
  §406.014(a)(7) now requires "the name and mailing address of the grantee". Correct option says "name and residence" and the why says "The statute asks for the grantee's name and residence". Update wording.
- **tx-rec-10** (Recordkeeping): A member of the public asks for a certified copy of an entry in your notary record and offers to pay the fee. You should:
  SOS adds a deadline: respond to a written request within 10 business days of receiving the fees (SOS FAQ; NV3 transcript). Could be mentioned.
- **tx-rec-11** (Recordkeeping): When a signer is identified by a credible witness, what goes in the record about that witness?
  Correct option says "name and residence or alleged residence"; §406.014(a)(5) now says "name and mailing address".
- **tx-rec-13** (Recordkeeping): If an instrument is proved by a subscribing witness, the record must include:
  Correct option says "the witness's residence"; §406.014(a)(6) now says "the mailing address of the witness".
- **tx-rec-14** (Recordkeeping): Which of these is NOT required in a Texas notary record entry?
  Stronger than "not required": 1 TAC §87.40 PROHIBITS recording the ID number or any other number that could identify the signer (SOS educational materials, Record Book note). Explanation could say so.
- **tx-seal-14** (Seal & Certificates): A document has no room for the notary's certificate and seal. What is the usual practice?
  Official sources confirm a notary may attach a certificate the signer chooses (SOS educational materials; NV2), but none says the attached certificate must describe the document. Sound practice, presented as the rule. Low priority.
- **tx-con-07** (Prohibited Acts & Liability): A notary's manager asks them to notarize a client's signature on a document left on the desk, without the client present. The notary should:
  Since Sept 1, 2025 knowingly notarizing for someone who did not personally appear is a crime: Class A misdemeanor, or state jail felony for real property (§406.0091). Explanation says only "serious violation"; could state the penalty.
- **tx-con-12** (Prohibited Acts & Liability): If the surety pays a claim on a notary's bond, what usually happens next?
  No official Texas source says the surety may seek repayment from the notary (this is general suretyship/indemnity law). SOS FAQ only says the bond is "a promise to pay for any financial loss due to the actions of a notary". Low priority.
- **tx-con-16** (Prohibited Acts & Liability): A Texas notary is convicted of a felony after being commissioned. What can happen?
  §406.004(b) says that if the SOS discovers a notary is not eligible, it SHALL revoke the commission (mandatory). The answer says "may revoke". Still the best option; wording could be firmer.
- **tx-spc-09** (Special Situations): For a country that is NOT part of the Hague Apostille Convention, what does the Texas Secretary of State issue for a notarized document?
  OUTDATED. The Texas SOS now issues a single "universal Apostille certificate, which acts as both an authentication and an apostille and is accepted in every country"; for non-Convention countries the next step is authentication by the U.S. State Department (SOS authinfo page). The answer "A certificate of authentication" and the "An apostille" wrong option need rethinking.
- **tx-onl-01** (Online Notarization): What must a person hold before applying to be a Texas online notary?
  §406.105(a): "A notary public OR an applicant for appointment as a notary public under Subchapter A may apply" for an online commission, so a current commission is not required before applying (the applicant must meet the Subchapter A qualifications, §406.105(b)(1)). The answer says one must hold a current commission first. Suggest: "A traditional Texas notary commission (or a pending application for one)".
- **tx-onl-07** (Online Notarization): What is 'credential analysis'?
  Statutory definition: a process "through which a third person affirms the validity of a government-issued identification credential through review of public and proprietary data sources" (§406.101(1)). The answer's "checking its security features" is a paraphrase; acceptable.

## New York (28 open)

- **ny-com-13** (Commission & Qualifications): A New York notary changes their name after marriage. What should they do?
  19 NYCRR §182.3(a)(8) sets a deadline: notify the Secretary of State within 5 days of a change of name or address. The DOS FAQ adds that the notary keeps notarizing under the commissioned name for the rest of the term. Could be stated.
- **ny-id-04** (Identification): A signer offers an expired driver's license and nothing else. The notary does not know them. The notary should:
  19 NYCRR §182.5(b)(1) requires an ID card that "is valid and current", so an expired card is not acceptable ID (other methods remain). Explanation says it "may not give that assurance"; could be firmer.
- **ny-id-09** (Identification): For an electronic notarial act, the notary does not personally know the remote signer. Which method satisfies identity under New York rules?
  19 NYCRR §182.5(c) requires ALL of: identity verification (remote presentation of a qualifying ID), credential analysis AND identity proofing. Answer lists only the last two. Still the best option.
- **ny-id-12** (Identification): An elderly signer has no ID, but the notary's long-time neighbor, whom the notary knows well, says she has known the signer for years. What should the notary consider?
  19 NYCRR §182.5(b)(4) expressly accepts "the oath or affirmation of a witness who is personally known to both the individual and notary" as satisfactory evidence. The answer frames it as something the notary must weigh; under the rule it is an enumerated method. Low priority.
- **ny-ack-03** (Acknowledgments & Proofs): What extra item must a certificate under Real Property Law §309-b (acknowledgment taken outside New York) contain?
  Could not confirm from the DOS booklet: its reprint of the §309-b form (p. 12) does not show the "(insert the city or other political subdivision and the state or country...)" line, and §309-b(6) there reads as if including that information is a permitted variance. nysenate.gov could not be checked. Needs a check against the statute text.
- **ny-jur-06** (Oaths, Affidavits & Depositions): For a jurat, when must the affiant sign?
  The DOS booklet requires the affiant to personally appear and take the oath before the notary, and the NY jurat reads "Sworn to before me" (no "subscribed"). It does not say the affidavit must be signed in the notary's presence. The answer states this as the rule. Same issue in ny-jur-11 and ny-jur-15. Low priority, but worth checking.
- **ny-jur-11** (Oaths, Affidavits & Depositions): An affiant arrives with an affidavit already signed and asks the notary to add a jurat. The notary should:
  See ny-jur-06: the booklet requires the oath in person; it does not require re-signing in the notary's presence. The answer ("have the affiant sign again") goes beyond the official source.
- **ny-jur-15** (Oaths, Affidavits & Depositions): Two affiants sign the same affidavit. What is the correct procedure?
  Each affiant must take their own oath ("an oath must be taken by an individual", booklet p. 21) — supported. "Have each sign in the notary's presence" is not in the official source (see ny-jur-06).
- **ny-fee-05** (Fees): What is the fee for protesting a note or bill of exchange for non-payment or non-acceptance?
  Citation corrected: the protest fee is in Exec. Law §135, not §136. Also, the explanation could give the actual notice fee: 10 cents per notice, up to five.
- **ny-rec-05** (Records): Why should a notary keep a record even when not strictly required?
  Premise "even when not strictly required" is outdated: 19 NYCRR §182.9 requires records for all notaries. Answer still sensible; question wording needs updating.
- **ny-seal-03** (Signature, Stamp & Authentication): What color ink does Executive Law §137 call for when signing and stamping?
  §137 requires the printed/typed/stamped information beneath the signature (and the NYC official number) to be in black ink; it does not say the signature itself must be black. The question says "when signing and stamping". Low priority.
- **ny-con-03** (Prohibited Acts & Misconduct): A non-attorney notary advertises notary services in Spanish. What does Executive Law §135-b require?
  §135-b(2) and 19 NYCRR §182.1(b) require the notice "in such other language" / "in the same language as the advertisement"; neither requires English as well. The disclaimer text is: "I am not an attorney licensed to practice law and may not give legal advice about immigration or any other legal matter or accept fees for legal advice." The answer says "in English and the other language". Suggest "in the language of the advertisement".
- **ny-con-11** (Prohibited Acts & Misconduct): A document has blank lines in the body. What should the notary do?
  No NY official source found for the blank-spaces rule (DOS booklet, FAQ and 19 NYCRR Part 182 are silent). Sound practice presented as a rule. Low priority.
- **ny-con-16** (Prohibited Acts & Misconduct): May a New York notary refuse to notarize?
  Worth noting: the DOS booklet (p. 15) also says an officer "is bound to administer [an oath] when requested, and a refusal to do so is a misdemeanor" (Penal Law §195.00). Refusal must be for a lawful reason.
- **ny-con-17** (Prohibited Acts & Misconduct): A notary refuses a customer because of the customer's national origin. Is this proper?
  The cited Exec. Law §296 (Human Rights Law) is not in the DOS notary materials and could not be checked (nysenate.gov blocks automated access). The booklet supports the duty to officiate on request. Answer is sound; citation replaced with the booklet.
- **ny-trm-02** (Legal Terms): What does 'ss.' (scilicet) mean in a venue?
  "Ss." is used in the DOS venue form ("State of New York, County of (New York) ss.:") but the DOS booklet does not define it. General legal definition; low priority.
- **ny-trm-03** (Legal Terms): What is a 'grantor'?
  "Grantor" is not defined in the DOS booklet (used in §290 and the disqualification examples). General legal definition; low priority.
- **ny-trm-04** (Legal Terms): What is a 'testator'?
  "Testator" is not defined in the DOS booklet. General legal definition; low priority.
- **ny-trm-08** (Legal Terms): What is a 'lessee'?
  "Lessee" is not defined in the DOS booklet (only "Lease"). General legal definition; low priority.
- **ny-trm-11** (Legal Terms): What is 'real property'?
  "Real property" is not defined in the DOS booklet. General legal definition; low priority.
- **ny-spc-05** (Special Situations): A signer can't speak English, and the notary doesn't speak the signer's language. What should the notary consider?
  No NY official source addresses language barriers or interpreters for notaries. The answer is reasonable practice (the notary must be satisfied of identity and willingness, §182.3), but presented as a rule. Low priority.
- **ny-spc-06** (Special Situations): A notary is asked to notarize a photograph. What should they do?
  No NY official source addresses notarizing photographs. Reasonable practice; low priority.
- **ny-spc-08** (Special Situations): Who appoints a 'commissioner of deeds' in New York City?
  Could not verify: the DOS booklet reprints only §140(14)-(15), which do not say who appoints NYC commissioners of deeds; the cited §§139–142 could not be checked (nysenate.gov blocks automated access). Needs a check against the statute text.
- **ny-spc-12** (Special Situations): A signer who can't write makes an 'X' mark as a signature. What is the prudent practice?
  No NY official source sets a procedure for signature by mark. The answer is prudent practice presented as the rule. Low priority.
- **ny-onl-04** (Electronic Notarization): If the electronic notary doesn't personally know the remote signer, what is required to identify them?
  As in ny-id-09: §182.5(c) requires identity verification (remote presentation of a qualifying ID), credential analysis AND identity proofing. Answer lists only the last two; still the best option.
- **ny-onl-09** (Electronic Notarization): Does registering as an electronic notary end the notary's ability to perform traditional paper notarizations?
  Answer is right (an electronic notary may also perform traditional acts), but per the DOS FAQ the e-notary license REPLACES the traditional one: "Once your e-notary license is issued, your traditional notary license will automatically be cancelled. You must use your new e-notary identification number." The explanation "an addition to the traditional commission" is imprecise.
- **ny-onl-12** (Electronic Notarization): What is 'identity proofing'?
  NY defines identity proofing as "a process by which a credential service provider collects, validates, and verifies information about a person" meeting NIST SP 800-63-3 Identity Assurance Level 2 (§§182.2(j), 182.7). The "knowledge-based questions" example is not in the NY rule (IAL2 does not rely on knowledge-based questions). Suggest dropping that example.
- **ny-onl-13** (Electronic Notarization): An electronic notary's computer holding their electronic signature is stolen. What should they do?
  NY rules require the notary to keep access information under sole control (§182.4(b)) but, unlike Texas, the DOS materials do not state a duty to notify the Department of State of theft. "Promptly notify the Department of State" is sensible but not a stated NY requirement. Low priority.

## California (20 open)

- **ca-com-13** (Commission & Qualifications): What is the civil penalty for willfully failing to notify the Secretary of State of an address change?
  §8213.5 makes willful failure to report an address change "punishable as an infraction by a fine of not more than $500" — a criminal infraction fine, not a "civil penalty" as the question, why and explanation say. Amount is right.
- **ca-com-14** (Commission & Qualifications): A notary legally changes their name. What must they do?
  §8213.6 sets no 30-day deadline for the name-change application; the 30 days runs from issue of the amended commission for filing the new oath and bond amendment, then 30 more days from filing to get a new seal (handbook p. 26, §8213). Willful failure to notify is an infraction up to $500. The answer's "File a name-change application with the Secretary of State within 30 days" misplaces the deadline.
- **ca-com-16** (Commission & Qualifications): Under Government Code §8202.8, what may an employer who paid for a notary's commission do?
  §8202.8 applies to a private employer that has an agreement with the notary under §8202.7 (paying the commission costs); the limit is "solely to transactions directly associated with the business purposes of the employer" during the ordinary course of employment. Answer is a fair paraphrase.
- **ca-id-13** (Satisfactory Evidence): Where must a notary record credible witnesses?
  For a single credible witness personally known to the notary, §8206(a)(2)(D) allows EITHER the witness's signature OR the details of the witness's ID in the journal; two credible witnesses must sign. "Including each credible witness's signature" is slightly too strict for the single-witness case. Low priority.
- **ca-id-15** (Satisfactory Evidence): What should a notary do if a signer's ID shows a slightly different name from the document?
  The handbook only says the best practice is to record the full name as shown on the ID (p. 27); it gives no rule on name mismatches. The answer is reasonable but presented as the rule. Low priority.
- **ca-ack-06** (Acknowledgments): May a California notary certify the signer's representative capacity (e.g. 'as president of XYZ Corp.')?
  Current §1189 says only that an out-of-state form may be used if it does not require the notary to "determine or certify that the signer holds a particular representative capacity"; the certificate recites "authorized capacity(ies)". The answer's "though the signer's capacity may be described for convenience" is not in the current statute or handbook. Low priority.
- **ca-ack-15** (Acknowledgments): A signer appears to lack understanding of the document. What should the notary do?
  No California official source states that a notary must be satisfied the signer understands or is acting willingly (the handbook covers communication with the signer, p. 23, but not capacity). Sound practice presented as a rule. Low priority.
- **ca-ack-16** (Acknowledgments): If an acknowledgment certificate is attached as a loose sheet, what is good practice?
  No California official source says a loose certificate must describe the document. Sound practice presented as the rule. Low priority.
- **ca-jur-03** (Jurats & Oaths): A document from another state contains a jurat that doesn't match California's wording. What must a California notary do?
  §8202(b) says "to any affidavit subscribed and sworn to before a notary, there shall be attached a jurat that includes" the boxed notice. It does not single out out-of-state jurats; the answer's "non-conforming jurat must be replaced" is an inference. Low priority.
- **ca-jur-07** (Jurats & Oaths): What is an affirmation?
  Cited CCP §2015.6 is not in the SOS handbook and could not be checked (leginfo blocks automated access); citation changed to CCP §§2093-2094, which the handbook reprints (affirmation "under penalty of perjury" as the alternative form).
- **ca-fee-10** (Fees): May a California notary charge a travel fee?
  The handbook only says "Travel fees, if any, should be identified, separately" in the journal (p. 11). The requirement that the signer agree in advance is not in the handbook or the reprinted statutes. Low priority.
- **ca-rec-15** (Journal): When is the journal entry made?
  §8206 does not state in words that the entry must be made at the time of the act (the handbook requires the certificate to be completed then). Sound practice. Low priority.
- **ca-seal-10** (Official Seal): The seal impression is smudged and illegible on a document. What should the notary do?
  The handbook stresses a clear, legible impression (p. 9) but gives no procedure for re-stamping a smudged seal. Answer is reasonable practice presented as guidance. Low priority.
- **ca-con-01** (Misconduct & Penalties): May a California notary who isn't an attorney give legal advice?
  Cited Bus. & Prof. Code §6125 is not reprinted in the SOS handbook and could not be checked (leginfo blocks automated access); ref points to the handbook's practice-of-law section instead.
- **ca-con-05** (Misconduct & Penalties): What is the consequence of a first violation of the 'notario' advertising rule?
  §8219.5(d): for a first offense the Secretary of State "shall suspend for a period of not less than one year OR revoke" the commission; a second offense means permanent revocation. The answer gives only the suspension. Low priority.
- **ca-con-09** (Misconduct & Penalties): What is the maximum civil penalty for negligently failing to obtain satisfactory evidence of identity?
  WRONG CITATION. §8214.15 sets $1,500/$750 penalties; the $10,000 civil penalty is in Civ. Code §1185(b)(1)(B), for a notary who fails to obtain the satisfactory evidence required for a credible witness under §1185(b)(1)(A). The statute does not say "negligently". Answer amount is supported; question wording ("negligently failing to obtain satisfactory evidence") should match the statute.
- **ca-spc-09** (Special Situations): A patient in a hospital seems confused. The family pushes for notarization. The notary should:
  No California official source addresses signer awareness or capacity. Sound practice presented as a rule. Low priority.
- **ca-spc-11** (Special Situations): May a notary use an interpreter to communicate with a signer?
  The handbook is firmer than the answer: "An interpreter should not be used, as vital information could be lost in the translation" (p. 23).
- **ca-spc-14** (Special Situations): May a notary certify a copy of their own journal entry?
  The handbook text describes supplying a photostatic copy of a journal line item to the public (§8206(c)) and furnishing certified copies of the journal to the Secretary of State on request (§8205(b)(1)); it does not describe a general power to "certify copies of journal entries" for anyone. Low priority.
- **ca-spc-15** (Special Situations): A signer wants a California notary to notarize via video call from Arizona. What applies?
  The handbook is definite: California notaries cannot currently perform remote online notarizations; the Online Notarization Act (SB 696) will not commence until at least January 1, 2030 (p. 27). The answer hedges ("if and as California law currently authorizes"); it could say plainly that California does not allow it.

## Florida (22 open)

- **fl-com-12** (Commission & Qualifications): A Florida notary changes their name. What must they do?
  §117.05(9) sets a deadline: request the amended commission within 60 days of the name change ($25 fee, notice of change form with signature specimen, bond rider); the notary may keep using the former name for 60 days or until the amended commission arrives. The answer omits the deadline. Low priority.
- **fl-id-06** (Identification): How many credible witnesses are needed if the witness personally knows both the notary and the signer?
  §117.05(5)(b)1 says one credible witness "personally known to the notary public" (who must personally know the signer). The question's wording "personally knows both the notary and the signer" is equivalent; fine.
- **fl-ack-01** (Acknowledgments): What is an acknowledgment in Florida?
  Florida statutes do not define an acknowledgment as a declaration that the signer signed "voluntarily"; §695.03 requires execution to be acknowledged by the party executing it. Wording is a reasonable paraphrase. Low priority.
- **fl-ack-12** (Acknowledgments): A document has no certificate and the signer asks which to use. The notary should:
  Florida sources list the unauthorized practice of law as a ground for suspension (§117.01(4)(f)) but do not say in words that the signer must choose the certificate. Reasonable inference; low priority.
- **fl-ack-13** (Acknowledgments): A signer under pressure from a relative tells the notary they don't want to sign. The notary should:
  No Florida official source addresses a signer acting under pressure; the FAQ only says a notary may refuse and must refuse in the situations in §§117.05 and 117.107. Sound practice; low priority.
- **fl-jur-10** (Oaths & Jurats): Who chooses oath or affirmation?
  No Florida source says in words that the signer chooses between oath and affirmation; §92.52 says an affirmation "may be substituted" for an oath. Sound practice; low priority.
- **fl-jur-12** (Oaths & Jurats): Which is a proper oath?
  The Governor's manual does not give an oath script for affidavits. Sound practice; low priority.
- **fl-fee-04** (Fees): Two people sign and acknowledge one deed. Florida charges per notarial act. What is the maximum?
  §117.05(2)(a) caps the fee "for any one notarial act". Whether two signers acknowledging on one certificate are one act or two is not addressed by the statute or manual. The $20 answer is an interpretation. Low-medium priority.
- **fl-fee-11** (Fees): May a Florida notary charge a separate travel fee?
